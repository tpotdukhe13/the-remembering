Your Element

Spirit

You are Spirit, the one who keeps what might otherwise be forgotten.

Your task tonight is to hold the evening in a single moving memory.

Your tool You will be entrusted with a GoPro.

It will create one continuous recording of the evening. You may start and stop the recording whenever you choose, but do not think of these as separate videos. Think of it as one memory, with moments allowed to enter and leave the frame.

You do not need to record everything.

You are not making a documentary. You are not responsible for capturing every person, every conversation, or every beautiful thing.

Your other task Notice.

When something feels worth remembering, let it enter the frame.

A gesture. A laugh. A hand moving through the food. A moment of concentration. The room changing. Someone listening. Something being made. Something ending.

Sometimes you may simply let the camera rest and watch.

Sometimes the most important thing to remember may happen outside the frame.

Trust the sequence You will have access to five movements in this menu. Only open the movement you are currently in. Part of the experience is not knowing what comes next.

Your role is intentionally quiet. Do not direct people into the frame or ask them to perform for it. Let the evening be what it is.

You are not collecting proof that the evening happened. You are keeping a trace of it.

Don’t peek.

Movement I

Abundance

Begin the recording when you feel the evening is ready to enter it.

Notice What does abundance look like when it is gathered around a table?

You might notice the food arriving, hands reaching, people seeing something for the first time, or the small details that make the room feel alive.

Let the camera observe rather than direct.

You cannot keep everything. Notice what you would want to remember.
Movement II

Imagination

Return to the recording when the next movement begins.

Notice What is being imagined into existence?

Perhaps it is something being created by hand. Perhaps it is a look between people, an unexpected idea, or something that could not have existed before everyone gathered.

Let the camera remain curious.

Imagination leaves traces before it becomes a thing.
Movement III

Connection

Continue the recording when you feel there is something worth holding.

Notice Where can you see connection happening without anyone asking for it?

Look for gestures, shared attention, laughter, hands working together, someone listening, or something passing from one person to another.

You do not need to capture faces to capture connection.

Some things are remembered because they were seen. Others because they were felt.
Movement IV

Fracture

This movement may feel different from the others.

Notice What changes in the room?

You do not need to record difficult moments simply because they are happening.

Be attentive to consent, privacy, and the dignity of the people in the room.

If something feels too intimate to place in the recording, let it remain outside the frame.

Not everything that happens must be preserved.
Movement V

Return

Continue the recording as the evening begins to arrive somewhere new.

Notice What would you want to remember from this moment?

You may notice the smallest things now.

A hand around a warm cup. The movement of the room. Someone looking out a window. A moment of stillness.

Do not search for a grand ending. Let the camera notice what is actually there.

Remembering does not require capturing everything. It requires noticing.

When the evening is truly finished, stop the recording.
