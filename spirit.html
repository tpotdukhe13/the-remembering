Your Element

Spirit

You are Spirit, the one who keeps what might otherwise be forgotten.

Your task tonight is to capture the evening in a series of moving memories.

Your tool

You will be entrusted with a Polaroid camera.

You are not making a documentary. You are not responsible for recording every person, every conversation, or every beautiful thing.Think of them as small pieces of memory, moments that are allowed to remain.

Your task

Notice.

When something feels worth remembering, let it become a photograph.

A gesture. A laugh. A hand moving through the food. A moment of concentration. The room changing. Someone listening. Something being made. Something ending.

Sometimes the most important thing to remember may not be the most obvious thing in the room.

Before we begin, spend five minutes in the kitchen to clarify your role and responsibilities for the experience. Once we begin, trust the sequence. Your instructions will be waiting when you need them.

A small rule
Take exactly two photographs in each movement. You do not need to explain why you chose them. Let the photographs hold what you noticed.

Trust the sequence. You will have access to five movements in this menu. Only open the movement you are currently in. Part of the experience is not knowing what comes next.

Before each movement begins, open the instructions. Read them before the movement starts so you know what to notice, what to do, and when to act. Then return to the experience.

Your role is intentionally quiet. Do not direct people into photographs or ask them to perform for the camera. Let the evening be what it is.

You are not collecting proof that the evening happened. You are keeping traces of it.

Don’t peek.

Movement I

Abundance

Take exactly two photographs during this movement.

Notice: What does abundance look like when it is gathered around a table?

You might notice the food arriving, hands reaching, people seeing something for the first time, or the small details that make the room feel alive.

As you photograph Let the camera observe rather than direct. Do not ask anyone to pose.

You cannot keep everything. Notice what you would want to remember.

Movement II

Imagination

Take exactly two photographs during this movement.

Notice: What is being imagined into existence?

Perhaps it is something being created by hand. Perhaps it is a look between people, an unexpected idea, or something that could not have existed before everyone gathered.

As you photograph Let creation keep you curious. Look for something that exists because someone imagined it first.

Imagination leaves traces before it becomes a thing. What can you create with the camera?

Movement III

Connection

Take exactly two photographs during this movement.

Notice: Where can you see connection happening without anyone asking for it?

Look for gestures, shared attention, laughter, hands working together, someone listening, or something passing from one person to another.

As you photograph You do not need to capture faces to capture connection.

Some things are remembered because they were seen. Others because they were felt.

Movement IV

Fracture

This movement may feel different from the others.

Take exactly two photographs during this movement.

Notice: What changes in the room?

As you photograph You do not need to photograph difficult moments simply because they are happening.

How do you capture an emotion? How do you capture intensity? How do you capture desctruction?

If something feels too intimate to place in a photograph, let it remain outside the frame.

Not everything that happens must be preserved.

Movement V

Return

Take exactly two photographs during this movement.

Notice: What would you want to remember from this moment?

You may notice the smallest things now.

A hand around a warm cup. The movement of the room. Someone looking out a window. A moment of stillness.

As you photograph Do not search for a grand ending. Let the camera notice what is actually there.

Remembering does not require capturing everything. It requires noticing.

You were an encapsulating example of Spirit tonight. Thank you for archiving us.
