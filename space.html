Your Element

Space

You are Space, the one who makes room for everything to exist.

The distance between stars. The pause between breaths. The room around a body. The silence between words.

Your role is simple: notice when something needs space.

A small rule Do not fill every opening. Sometimes the most important thing you can create is room for something to remain exactly as it is.

You will be entrusted with a small bucket and a bell for the evening.

Whenever you ring the bell, it is an invitation for the room to become attentive.

Your other task Watch the room.

Notice when Nature needs physical room, when a conversation needs room to unfold, when someone needs room to be heard, or when silence simply needs to exist.

If space is needed, create it gently. Move something. Pause something. Leave something unsaid. Let someone finish. Or simply do nothing.

Trust the sequence You will have access to five movements in this menu. Only open the movement you are currently in. Part of the experience is not knowing what comes next.

Your role is intentionally small. You do not need to manage the evening, explain your element, or anticipate what anyone else needs from you.

When your instruction is complete, return to the experience and be a guest.

Don’t peek.

Movement I

Abundance

Before Nature begins the story, ring the bell.

Then become attentive to the room.

Notice Is there anything that needs more space to exist?

It may be physical space, conversational space, or simply a little more time.

If you notice something that needs room, create it gently.

Abundance does not require everything to occupy the same space.
Movement II

Imagination

Before Nature begins the story, ring the bell.

As the experience unfolds, pay attention to what is beginning to emerge.

Notice Is there something here that needs room to become what it might be?

Protect that space if you can.

Do not rush to interpret, explain, or fill it.

Possibility needs somewhere to live.
Movement III

Connection

Before Nature begins the story, ring the bell.

Listen closely as the room begins to move through connection.

Notice Does anyone or anything need more room to be present?

If a conversation wants to continue, let it continue.

If someone needs to be heard, make room for them.

If nothing needs to be added, allow the silence to remain.

Connection is not always closeness. Sometimes it is the room we give each other.
Movement IV

Fracture

Before Nature begins the story, ring the bell.

This movement may carry more weight than the others.

Notice Where does the room need space to hold what has just been said?

Do not hurry to soften the moment.

Do not rush to make anyone comfortable.

If silence arrives, protect it.

If someone needs to speak, make room for them.

Not every silence needs to be broken.
Movement V

Return

Before Nature begins the story, ring the bell.

Then notice the room as it is now.

Notice What needs space now?

Perhaps there is something left to say.

Perhaps there is something that should remain unsaid.

Perhaps the room simply needs to become quiet enough to notice what has changed.

Make room for whatever is here.

Then return to the experience.
